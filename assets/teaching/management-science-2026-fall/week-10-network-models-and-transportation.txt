Graphs and Networks

                                                                                         Graph
 GRAPH = (NODES, EDGES)
 A graph represents the pattern of connections
 between nodes.

 NETWORK = (NODES, EDGES, ATTRIBUTES)
                                                                                         Weighted network
 A network adds information about nodes or edges.
 It helps us study flows and identify important nodes.

 Categorical: gender, occupation, region, date, type
 Numerical: strength, cost, distance, time, capacity

Types of Network Problems


 Facility layout / design                      Arrange or connect facilities, such as machines or base stations.


 Routing                                       Find the best route between an origin and a destination.


 Flow optimization                             Determine how much traffic or material to send through a network.


 Transportation                                Ship directly from supply locations to demand locations.


 Transshipment                                 Ship through intermediate locations or distribution hubs.


 Assignment                                    Assign N workers or machines to N tasks, one to one.

Example 6-3: Maximum Flow Problem




 A dam (H) supplies water to cities 1–5 through
 a pipeline network. An earthquake has
 destroyed the water-storage facility in city 5.

 Determine the maximum amount of water that
 can be sent from the dam to city 5.

 The numbers indicate capacities per unit of
 time; a capacity of 0 means that flow is not
 allowed in that direction.
