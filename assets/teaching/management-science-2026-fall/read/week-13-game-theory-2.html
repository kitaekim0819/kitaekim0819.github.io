Example 10-7: The Prisoner’s Dilemma

 Kim and Lee are accomplices who have been arrested and placed in separate cells. While awaiting
 trial, each can confess or remain silent. Their choices determine the prison sentences below.




                                Lee                  Confess                             Remain silent
   Kim
   Confess                                             (4, 4)                                (1, 8)

   Remain silent                                       (8, 1)                                (2, 2)


 Entries are prison terms in years (Kim, Lee). Each player prefers a shorter sentence.

Example 10-6: Price Competition
 A prisoner’s dilemma occurs when individually optimal choices lead to an outcome that is worse for both
 players than mutual cooperation.

 S Electronics and L Electronics sell TVs. At regular prices, each earns KRW 100 billion. If only one
 discounts, it earns KRW 200 billion while its rival loses KRW 50 billion. If both discount, neither earns
 a profit.

                                         L         Regular price                 Discount price
   S
   Regular price                                       (1, 1)                        (−0.5, 2)

   Discount price                                    (2, −0.5)                        (0, 0)


 Payoff pairs (S, L). Profit in KRW 100 billion.

Example 10-11: The Partnership Dilemma

 Kim and Lee jointly open a small restaurant. Each can focus on the restaurant or take an outside
 part-time job.


                                    Lee            Focus on restaurant                  Take outside job
   Kim
   Focus on restaurant                                      (5, 5)                            (2, 4)

   Take outside job                                         (4, 2)                            (3, 3)


 If both focus on the restaurant, each expects KRW 50 million per year, although success is uncertain. If one takes
 an outside job, that person expects KRW 40 million and the partner KRW 20 million.

 If both take outside jobs, each expects KRW 30 million. Units: KRW 10 million/year.

Example 10-10: The Free-Rider Dilemma

 Kim and Lee meet for dinner. Before eating, each decides whether to contribute to the KRW 40,000
 bill or refuse to pay.


                                Lee                Contribute                           Refuse to pay
   Kim
   Contribute                                          (2, 2)                                (4, 0)

   Refuse to pay                                       (0, 4)                              (10, 10)

 If both contribute, each pays KRW 20,000. If only one contributes, that person pays the full bill and the other
 eats for free.

 If neither pays, dinner is canceled. The disappointment is valued at KRW 100,000 each. Entries are costs in KRW
 10,000; lower is better.

Example 10-8: Capacity Expansion

 A and B supply an advanced electronic component worldwide. Each holds 50% of the market and
 earns KRW 800 billion per year. Both are considering adding a production line.


 If only one expands, its profit rises and its rival’s falls. If both expand, excess capacity lowers
 utilization and severely reduces profitability.


                                         B            Maintain capacity                 Expand
   A
   Maintain capacity                                        (8, 8)                      (4, 16)

   Expand                                                  (16, 4)                       (2, 2)
 Payoff pairs (A, B). Annual profit in KRW 100 billion.

Example 10-9: Moral Hazard (Deadlock)

 Kim and Lee run civil engineering businesses and plan to bid for a new government project. Each can
 follow the normal process or seek improper favors from a government official.


 If both follow the normal process, each has a 30% chance of winning. If only one seeks favors, the
 chances become 60% for that bidder and 10% for the other. If both seek favors, each has a 40% chance.


                                       Lee                 Normal process          Seek improper favors
   Kim
   Normal process                                               (30, 30)                 (10, 60)

   Seek improper favors                                         (60, 10)                 (40, 40)


 Entries are probabilities of winning the contract (%), in the order (Kim, Lee).

Example 10-12: Challenging Wrongdoing

 New employees Kim and Lee discover wrongdoing at their company. Each can accept the
 situation or protest.


                                               Lee                          Accept          Protest
   Kim
   Accept                                                                     (3, 3)         (4, 2)

   Protest                                                                    (2, 4)         (5, 5)

 The narrative says both receive satisfaction 3 if they accept. A lone protester faces difficulties and receives
 satisfaction 2. If both protest, the wrongdoing is resolved and both receive satisfaction 5.


 Source inconsistency: the (Protest, Accept) cell is (4, 2), unlike the narrative.
