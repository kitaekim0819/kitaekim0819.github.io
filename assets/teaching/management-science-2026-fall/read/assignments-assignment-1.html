Problem 1
Company C produces Products 1 and 2. Each unit of Product 1 earns a profit of KRW 40,000,
and each unit of Product 2 earns a profit of KRW 30,000. The company wants to maximize its
total daily profit.

Each unit of Product 1 requires 2 machine-hours and 1 labor-hour. Each unit of Product 2
requires 1 machine-hour and 1 labor-hour. The company has 100 machine-hours and 80 labor-
hours available per day. All units produced can be sold.

Formulate a linear programming model to maximize total daily profit.

Problem 2
A workshop produces Products X and Y. Quantities x and y are continuous and nonnegative. Its
weekly profit, in units of KRW 10,000, is described by the following model:

                            Maximize     3x + 2y

                            subject to

                              x + y ≤ 70 (labor hours)

                              2x + y ≤ 100 (machine hours)

                              x, y ≥ 0

Solve this model graphically. Show the feasible region and identify the unique optimal
solution and the optimal objective value on your graph.

Problem 3
F Electronics produces standard amplifiers (s) and high-performance amplifiers (h). Each
standard amplifier earns KRW 30,000 in profit, and each high-performance amplifier earns KRW
50,000. Quantities are continuous and nonnegative. The daily profit-maximization model is:

                     Maximize     3s + 5h (unit: KRW 10,000)

                     subject to

                             s + 2h ≤ 180 (assembly hours)

                             s + h ≤ 140 (testing hours)

                             h ≤ 45 (semiconductor chips)

                             s, h ≥ 0

Consider the production plan s = 100 and h = 40, with continuous quantities. (1) Calculate the
unused amount of each resource (i.e. slack variables), (2) use it to classify each resource
constraint as binding or nonbinding, and (3) discuss the managerial implications of the
slack variables and binding or nonbinding constraints.

Problem 5
Consider the following two models. In both models, x and y are continuous and nonnegative.

                     Model A

                            Maximize     2x + y

                            subject to   x + y ≤ 5,

                                         x ≥ 4,

                                         y≥3

                     Model B

                            Maximize     2x + y

                            subject to   x − y ≤ 4,

                                         x ≥ 0,

                                         y≥0

A student claims, “Neither model has a finite optimal solution, so both models must be
infeasible.” Evaluate this claim by giving the correct status of each model and explaining
the mathematical distinction.

Problem 9
Company K offers Company Y up to 120 additional molding hours at KRW 30,000 per hour;
fractional hours may be purchased. The original unit profits already deduct an in-house variable
molding cost of KRW 20,000 per hour, which is avoided for each outsourced hour. Outsourcing
adds usable molding capacity and changes no other original model data.

Molding capacity is originally 700 hours. Its current shadow price remains valid when total
molding capacity is between 500 and 750 hours, with all other data fixed.

(1) Calculate the net additional cost and marginal net benefit when Company Y outsource
one hour of molding to Company K. (2) Recommend the largest number of hours to
outsource and calculate the resulting total profit gain.
