Game Theory: Key Terms

 • Player: a decision maker who chooses a strategy and receives a
         payoff.
         • A player may be an individual, a firm, or a country.
 • Payoff: the gain or loss from a combination of strategies, expressed in
         money or utility and summarized in a payoff matrix

Game Theory: Types of Games

 • Types by number of players: two-person or n-person games.
 • Types by sum of payoff
   • Zero-sum: total payoff is zero. One player’s gain equals another’s
      loss.
   • Non-zero-sum: payoffs need not sum to zero. Joint gains or losses
      are possible.
 • Others
   • cooperative vs. non-cooperative
   • simultaneous vs. sequential.

Game Theory: Solution Concepts

 • A solution predicts or prescribes the strategies that players choose.
         • Dominant-strategy solution: each player chooses a strategy that performs better than their
               alternatives regardless of others’ choices. Such a solution need not exist.
         • Maximin solution: choose the strategy with the highest worst-case payoff. This is a
               conservative criterion and a standard approach in two-person zero-sum games.
         • Nash equilibrium: no player can improve their payoff by changing only their own strategy,
               given the others’ strategies.
                 • Best response strategy: a strategy that maximizes a player’s payoff given others’
                       strategies. At a Nash equilibrium, all players choose best responses.

Summary

 • Players choose strategies and receive payoffs. A solution describes their choices.
 • A dominant strategy performs better regardless of the opponent’s choice.
 • At a Nash equilibrium, all players choose best responses, and no unilateral change
       improves a player’s payoff.
 • Maximin selects the highest worst-case payoff.
 • Pure strategies choose one action. Mixed strategies assign probabilities to actions.
 • In non-zero-sum games, total payoffs need not equal zero.
